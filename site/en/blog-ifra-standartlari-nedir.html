What are IFRA standards? Which documents to ask your fragrance supplier for

In space scenting the fragrance matters as much as the device. One of the first questions put to a fragrance supplier in business purchasing is whether the product complies with IFRA standards. So what is IFRA, and how is compliance documented?

What is IFRA?

IFRA (the International Fragrance Association) is the industry body that represents the fragrance sector worldwide and aims to promote the safe use of fragrance materials. IFRA has two main tools:

  • IFRA Standards: published when there is a concern about the safe use of a particular fragrance material. A standard can ban a material, limit how much may be used or set purity requirements. These decisions are based on safety assessments by RIFM (the Research Institute for Fragrance Materials), an independent research body.
  • IFRA Code of Practice: the commitment to good practice that IFRA members follow when making and handling fragrance materials and mixtures. Members must ensure that the products they supply are safe for their intended use and comply with applicable legislation.

IFRA standards set different limits by product category; the same material may be used at different levels in a perfume, a candle and an air freshener.

How is compliance documented?

A fragrance's compliance with IFRA standards is usually shown with an IFRA certificate of conformity issued by the manufacturer. It states the maximum level at which the mixture may be used in each product category. Check which version (amendment) of the standards the certificate refers to; the standards are updated over time.

Documents to request in business purchasing

  1. Safety data sheet (SDS): the fragrance's hazard classification and storage, transport and first-aid information.
  2. IFRA certificate of conformity: the categories and levels suitable for air fresheners and diffusers.
  3. Allergen declaration: the list of allergenic fragrance materials in the mixture that must be declared.
  4. Test reports: independent laboratory test reports, if any, and which product each report covers.
