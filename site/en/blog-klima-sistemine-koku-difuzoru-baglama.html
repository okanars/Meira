Connecting a scent diffuser to the HVAC system: a guide

In large or partitioned spaces such as hotel lobbies, shopping centres, airports and convention centres, spreading scent with individual devices is difficult. The most even method in these buildings is to feed the scent into the airflow the building already has: the HVAC system.

How it works

An HVAC-connected diffuser does not sit in the space itself but in the plant room, above a suspended ceiling or in a technical room. It breaks the fragrance into very fine, dry particles and feeds them into the ventilation duct through a hose or pipe. The scent then reaches the space through all the vents with the airflow in the duct. Because condensation and build-up in the ducts are undesirable, such devices usually use compressed-air technology that produces dry particles without water or heat. The product pages state which atomisation method each HVAC-connected model in the catalogue uses.

Connection point

There are two common approaches in the industry:

  • Return air duct: the scent is fed into the air returning to the air handling unit. It passes through the unit and reaches all supply vents.
  • Air handling unit (AHU) outlet: the device doses the supply air just after the air handling unit.

Which is suitable depends on the type of unit, the position of the filters and the duct layout. This decision should be made together with the person responsible for the building's mechanical installation.

What to gather before a project

  1. The total volume of the areas to be scented and the floor plan.
  2. The number and location of air handling units and which areas they serve.
  3. The distance from the return and supply ducts to where the device will go.
  4. The operating hours of the air handling unit (scent spreads while the system is running).
  5. A power supply for the device and an accessible place for refilling.

Maintenance and operation

  • Timing: the device's operating hours should match those of the air handling unit; scent does not spread through the ducts while the system is off.
  • Refills: larger reservoirs reduce how often you refill. Fragrance use depends on the intensity setting and running time.
  • Checks: in the first weeks, check the intensity at different vents and adjust.
