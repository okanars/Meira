Sustainable space scenting

The environmental impact of scenting depends largely on three things: how much fragrance is used, how long packaging and devices stay in use, and how batteries and devices are collected at the end of their life. This page brings together what the products offer on these points and what to watch for in practice.

The right size, less fragrance

A device that is too powerful for the volume of a space uses more fragrance than needed; one that is too weak ends up running at its highest setting with the same result. The first step towards sustainable scenting is choosing a device whose coverage suits the air volume of the space.

The volume calculator works out the volume from the floor area and ceiling height and lists the models that cover it, starting with the closest coverage.

Run it only when needed

Most diffusers in the catalogue offer timed spraying; on app-enabled models the operating hours and spray and pause times are set from a phone. Running the device only while the space is open, and spraying in intervals rather than continuously, directly cuts fragrance use.

Intermittent operation also helps perception: people quickly get used to a scent they are constantly exposed to. More fragrance does not always mean more perception. More on this: Scent fatigue and the right intensity.

Refills and packaging

Refill fragrances are supplied in bottles and larger containers, and as cartridges for the ultrasonic models. In large spaces and businesses with many devices, larger refill volumes usually mean less packaging for the same amount of fragrance. The devices are refillable; they are not a single-use cartridge system thrown away with the reservoir.

The list of refill fragrances will appear on the site once the fragrance collection is confirmed.

Long device life and maintenance

The most sustainable device is one that stays in use for a long time. On the ultrasonic wall models (CK-628, CK-638) the atomisation plate can be replaced; when it wears, only this part is renewed rather than the whole device. Regular cleaning and the right fragrance extend the life of nozzles and plates.

  • Use the device with the fragrance type recommended by the manufacturer.
  • Empty the reservoir if the device will not be used for a long time.
  • If it breaks down, ask for technical support before throwing it away: technical support request.

Energy and power supply

Professional diffusers run on little power. According to the manufacturer, the ultrasonic wall models (CK-628, CK-638, CK-648) draw 2 W. They can run on 4 AA batteries or a Type-C 6V supply; for continuous use a mains supply creates less waste than frequent battery changes. Some tabletop models (for example ADS-622) charge via Type-C.

Batteries and electronic waste

Batteries and electronic devices at the end of their life must not be thrown away with household waste.

  • Waste batteries: in Türkiye, portable waste batteries are collected through the Portable Battery Manufacturers and Importers Association (TAP), authorised by the Ministry of Environment, Urbanisation and Climate Change. Used batteries can be left in TAP collection boxes at municipalities, schools and shops.
  • Electronic devices: waste electrical and electronic equipment falls under the Regulation on the Management of Waste Electrical and Electronic Equipment, published in the Official Gazette on 26 December 2022 and in force since 1 February 2023. Take these devices to municipal waste collection centres or authorised collection points.

Indoor air quality

Fragrance products contain volatile organic compounds (VOCs) that evaporate at room temperature. The US Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) lists air fresheners among indoor VOC sources and recommends increasing ventilation and following the directions for use when using products that emit VOCs. In space scenting this means not using more scent than needed and making sure the ventilation of the space is working.

Fragrance content

According to the manufacturer, its fragrances are made from natural plant extracts free of allergens, toxins and carcinogens; raw materials are sourced from the USA and France and compliance with IFRA standards has been tested by SGS. These are manufacturer statements; you can request the safety data sheet (SDS) and compliance documents during the quote process. We explain what IFRA is in this article.

What this page does not cover

This page gives no figures for the carbon footprint of the products, their recycled material content or the recyclability of their packaging. This information will be added once it is received from the manufacturer as verified data.
