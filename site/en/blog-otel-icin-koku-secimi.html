Choosing scent for a hotel: scent families for the lobby, corridors and rooms

In hotels, scent is one of the first details a guest encounters. A well-planned scent strategy brings the different areas of a hotel together under one identity while keeping each area at an intensity suited to its use.

One signature scent or different scents by area?

Many hotels are known for a "signature scent" used in the lobby. Continuing it at a lower intensity in corridors and lifts helps guests experience the hotel as a whole. Areas such as the spa and restaurant are treated separately according to their function: in the restaurant, a very light scent that does not compete with the food, or none at all, is preferred.

Choosing a scent family

  • Lobby: fresh, familiar notes such as green tea, white tea and white amber; they are unlikely to bother a wide audience.
  • Evenings and the bar: scents with a woody base such as bergamot, sandalwood or cedar.
  • Spa and rooms: calm notes such as lavender, chamomile, white musk or sage.

Tips for putting it into practice

  1. Try samples first. Test the scent in the real space with the device, not on a paper strip; the same scent is perceived differently in different volumes.
  2. Ask the staff. The team who work in the same setting all day give valuable feedback on intensity.
  3. Mount the device above eye level. Wall devices should be placed close to the airflow and at an accessible height.
  4. Adjust by the hour. Intensity can be raised at busy check-in and check-out times and lowered at night.
