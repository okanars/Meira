Choosing a scent diffuser by room volume: the m³ calculation step by step

The first figure to check when choosing a scent diffuser is its coverage. Manufacturers usually give it in cubic metres (m³), because scent fills a volume of air, not a floor. Of two spaces with the same floor area, the one with the higher ceiling needs a stronger device.

How to calculate volume

The calculation is simple: floor area (m²) x ceiling height (m) = air volume (m³).

Example calculations

Meeting room40 m²2.8 m112 m³
Boutique shop150 m²3.2 m480 m³
Hotel lobby400 m²6 m2,400 m³
Shopping centre atrium2,000 m²4 m8,000 m³

According to the manufacturer, the devices in the catalogue cover a wide range, from small plug-in models to twin-head series connected to the HVAC system. A few examples:

  • CK-628 ultrasonic wall diffuser: 300 m³ (meeting rooms, hotel rooms, offices).
  • CK-688 dual-fluid wall diffuser: 800 m³ (shops, showrooms).
  • ADS-687 column diffuser: 1,500-2,000 m³ (lobbies, restaurants).
  • CK-621 / 622 / 623 series: 8,000 m³, with 1000 / 3000 / 5000 ml reservoir options (large spaces, HVAC connection).

When the calculation is not enough

Coverage is an upper limit given for an open, undivided volume. In real spaces, the following may call for more capacity or more than one device:

  • Partitioned spaces. Walls and doors interrupt the airflow. Rooms opening onto corridors, mezzanines and galleries should be treated as separate volumes.
  • Strong ventilation. Where the air changes often (areas near a restaurant kitchen, entrance doors that open frequently), scent disperses faster.
  • Heavy visitor traffic. Crowds can lower the perceived intensity of the scent.
  • Very high ceilings. Warm air collects at the top, so the position of the device (height, closeness to the airflow) matters more.

In large and partitioned spaces, a device connected to the building's ventilation system can give a more even result than a single powerful unit. We cover this in detail in our HVAC connection guide.

A short checklist

  1. Measure the floor area and ceiling height and calculate the volume.
  2. Choose a model whose coverage comfortably meets that volume.
  3. Note whether the space is partitioned and what HVAC infrastructure it has.
  4. Check the device's noise level against the use of the space (a meeting room and a shopping centre have different expectations).
  5. Estimate how often it needs refilling from the fragrance capacity.
