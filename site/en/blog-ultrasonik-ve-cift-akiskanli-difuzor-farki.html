Ultrasonic versus dual-fluid (nano) diffusers: what is the difference?

Professional scent diffusers release fragrance into the air in two main ways: ultrasonic vibration or compressed air. Neither uses heat; the difference lies in how the particles are formed and what follows from that.

Ultrasonic atomisation

In ultrasonic devices, fragrance turns into a fine mist on a plate (ceramic disc) vibrating at high frequency. Most home aroma diffusers use this principle to turn water and fragrance into mist without boiling them. According to the manufacturer, the ultrasonic wall models in the catalogue (CK-628, CK-638, CK-648) run below 8 dBA and have a replaceable ultrasonic plate.

Strengths: very quiet operation, low power use, compact body. Limits: smaller coverage; the ultrasonic wall models in the catalogue cover 300 m³. The plate can wear over time and need replacing.

Dual-fluid (compressed air) atomisation

In these devices a small air pump blows air at high speed across the fragrance, tearing it into very fine particles that mix with the air. No water is used and the fragrance is spread undiluted. The industry also calls this a "nebuliser" or "dry mist". According to the manufacturer, the dual-fluid systems in the catalogue reduce the particles to nano scale with an ionised accelerator; because very fine particles bounce off surfaces without breaking, they leave no wet residue.

Strengths: larger coverage (in the catalogue, from 300 m³ plug-in models to 15,000 m³ twin-head series), dense and even distribution, and HVAC connection. Limits: because of the pump they are more audible than ultrasonic models; since the fragrance is not diluted, the intensity setting matters more.

Which should you choose?

  • Meeting rooms, hotel rooms, surgeries, small offices: ultrasonic models if noise is the priority.
  • Shops, showrooms, restaurants, lobbies: dual-fluid models if coverage and intensity are the priority.
  • Very large or partitioned spaces: dual-fluid devices connected to the HVAC system.
